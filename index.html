Projects

Two team projects from this semester, then two course assignments. Each page says what I did myself and what help I had.

  • HillPark

    INEG 22203 Computing Methods II, fall 2026. Team of three.

    A web app that tells a University of Arkansas student where to park for their class schedule. It ranks the lots your permit allows by walking time, driving time and traffic, checks whether a park-and-ride bus would be faster, and uses an integer program to pick the best lots for your whole week. Its score is minutes of walking and traffic delay, plus a penalty for lots likely to be full.

    • 16%lower week score with two lots instead of one (178 to 149)
    • 35automated tests, including brute-force checks of the week plan
    • 0.51R² of the traffic forecast on unseen days, vs 0.32 for averages
    Read about HillPark →

  • Campus parking permit model

    INEG 26103 Introduction to Operations Research, fall 2026. Team of two.

    The university sells more car permits than it has spaces. We're building a mixed-integer model that decides which permit each campus lot should serve, to shorten walks without sending everyone down the same road. Approved at the October milestone; results come in December.

    • 1.44car permits sold for every parking space
    • 110lots and garages in our dataset, with permit rules and sizes
    • 87.4%unsatisfied with parking in a 2024 campus study
    Read about the permit model →

  • Four ways to solve one shipping problem

    INEG 22203, Assignment 5. Individual.

    One transportation problem solved as an integer program, a constraint program, a linear program and with a greedy heuristic I wrote. Only 15 suppliers and 15 outlets had anything to ship, so trimming the empty ones would cut the model from 25 million variables to 225.

    • 1.0%above optimal for my greedy heuristic, in 19 ms
    • 7×faster as a linear program than with SCIP, same optimum
